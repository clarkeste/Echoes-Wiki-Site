[[Hyrule|Hyrule]], along with other advancing countries such as [[Holodrum|Holodrum]], have a **burgeoning peasant merchant class**. These peasants are not officially recognized by the [[Last King of Hyrule|King]] in the same way the **Noblemen** are, nor do they have hereditary land that is bound to their family line.

These merchants have been called ‘capitalists’, or the educated class. Whereas the wealth of the nobility is almost entirely assured due to their hereditary ownership of land, these capitalists instead base their wealth on ‘capital’. Capital refers to any other form of asset ownership, if it be pure [[Rupees|Rupees]], or the ownership of businesses and other properties.

While in ages before, it was expected that the noble lord of the region had ultimate ownership over any business constructed by peasants on their land, land reform following the [[First Hyrulean Civil War|Hyrulean Civil War]] slowly started to erode this concept. This was due to the growth of new, metropolitan cities like [[Castle Town|Hyrule Castle Town]], that became centers of trade, instead of the Kingdom’s [[Economy|economy]] being dominated by large swathes of rural land.

Over the course of years, new laws and decrees made it clear that peasants or nobles who founded their own enterprises, had ultimate ownership over them, unless sold or otherwise lost. This, along with the loss of decentralized noble power following the Hyrulean Civil War, increased the power of the educated capitalist class.

The ascension of peasants to noblemen is rare for a variety of reasons. However, any peasant is—legally—able to become a wealthy merchant. Despite this promise, it is not possible in practice for all regular civilians. This is due to various economic factors, such as the concentration of wealth, and other such things.

While some laud the existence of the educated class as evidence of a new system where meritocracy reigns, others believe that wealth is inherently corrupting and that the capitalists are no better than the noblemen. Some others fall in-between. Personally, this author believes that while perhaps a necessary step forward, the ambition of these merchants will destabilize society in such a way that the dependable nobility wouldn’t have.

Noble sentiment towards these new economic truths is one of frustration, as this author can attest. This ‘new money’ is often seen as vain, temporary, and privileged above their station. However, there is increasingly less difference between minor noblemen and wealthy merchants, legally-speaking. Indeed, while these concepts continue to exist, many of the wealthiest among the educated class own land, and many Nobles find themselves resigned to sell their land to make ends meet. The world is changing.

Several vestiges of this old system remain, however. For instance, taxes are still paid to the land owner—the nobleman who owns the lands—who then pays a share of it to the [[Former Hyrulean Government and Royal Family|central government]]. Despite the attempts of the new [[The Followers of Dragmire, Revolutionaries|Revolutionary government]] to upend this tradition and have taxes be paid directly to the government, [[Lakoh|Lakoh]]’s faction has so far refused to agree and blocked this measure.

In [[Castle Town|Castle Town]] and some other cities ‘owned’ by the crown, taxes are paid directly to the [[Last King of Hyrule|King]]—or now, are owned by the public and taxes are paid to the government. Additionally, [[Autonomy Clause|autonomous zones]] like Kakariko City do not operate under this paradigm at all. Other cities like [[Castor City|Castor]] or [[Akkala City|Akkala]] have no Lord, although many nobles who own land around it live inside the city. Instead, these cities have a Governor that is elected or appointed in various different ways. Governors come and go, and are not from a particular familial line; instead, these cities have their own local government that reports back to the central government.

While feudalism is technically still present due to nobles owning land, it is essentially no longer a relevant matter to discuss in [[The Hylians|Hylian]] society. Even prior to the [[First Hyrulean Civil War|first Hyrulean Civil War]], all peasants had the right to travel—a rarity in eras past, but now the law of the land everywhere. There are no border checkpoints between domains, there is a largely unified law enforcement system, and other such inconveniences of the system have been ironed away. Over time, nobles who control land no longer really govern that land; they only have special economic rights in the area and benefit monetarily from the land’s exploitation or use.

This was a core issue of what initially started the first Hyrulean Civil War, as the nobility wished for more independence and to be able to truly govern their lands. However, with the victory of the King in the Hyrulean Civil War, most have accepted that it’s a fight that is not worth starting again, even if some noblemen grumble about it to the modern day.

Despite all this, noblemen are still usually very wealthy due to their station in society. The hereditary land system means that the nobility benefits from simply owning land given to them by birth, without really having to do anything. Many nobles have used this vast amount of wealth they have inherited to play the games of the peasants, and accumulate capital, becoming powerful in that way as well.
