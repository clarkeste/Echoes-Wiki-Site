**The Gorons** are a large, mountain-dwelling people, who in [[Hyrule|Hyrule]] live primarily on and around [[Death Mountain|Death Mountain]]. They are largely considered a humble and relaxed people, gentle despite their size. While they are slow, they can roll into a ball to move faster. Their hard skin and rocky back lets them roll even on rough terrain without being bruised or cut.

Following the beginning of the [[Revolution and Second Civil War|Revolution]], and as [[The Followers of Dragmire, Revolutionaries|Follower]] armies closed in on [[Castle Town]], the Gorons chose to announce their neutrality in the [[Revolution and Second Civil War|current Civil War]]. Death Mountain and various nearby areas, such as [[Kakariko Village]], are included in a neutral zone.

While the Gorons have become more wary of outsiders since the Civil War, they are still kind to travelers. Refugees and visitors to Death Mountain and Goron City have become more common, seeking a respite from hate and war. Despite growing concerns about housing and integration for all of the new arrivals, the Goron council and most Gorons have chosen to remain kind and warm to these refugees.

While a tourist destination before, its extremely hot climate dissuaded many. However, after choosing neutrality in the Civil War, Death Mountain has become a hub both for traders, and the wary who have been hurt or dislocated by the war.

The primary food of the Gorons are rocks. Usually, the rocks are cooked above red-hot stones, or in lava; creating a Rock Roast, one of the most beloved foods in Goron culture. Of these rocks, Basalt is usually considered the best, but is quite rare. In addition to water, they also drink lava and make soups with it. Gemstones are nearly universally considered to be a yucky food by the Gorons; supposedly, before they realized that the other peoples of Hyrule would pay top [[Rupees|Rupee]] for it, they used to throw whatever gemstones they gained into the lava of Death Mountain, believing them to be useless. 

Gorons live an average of a little over one hundred years, and reproduce asexually—this additionally means there is no female gender for the Gorons. Whether Gorons should be considered male or genderless is a series of debate among scholars. Gorons themselves usually refer to themselves as male, but some chose not to. Gorons often call each other 'brothers' if they were born in the same cave at similar times, and 'descendants' if the line of adoption can be traced back to an 'ancestor'.

With their [[Autonomy Clause|autonomy]] having been violated less than the [[The Zora|Zora]] or [[Kakariko City]], the Gorons had little reason to be involved in the [[Revolution and Second Civil War|Revolution]]. While individual Gorons chose to help the Followers, Goron society at large did not get involved. Therefore, Death Mountains acts as a zone devoid of both [[The Followers of Dragmire, Revolutionaries|Follower]] and [[The Royalists|Royalist]] soldiers; although spies and diplomats alike both roam Goron City.

The Gorons, not wanting to be enablers of violence or cause their home to become simply a launchpad for war profiteering, have banned the sale of weapons to [[The Royalists|Royalists]] or Followers on their land. This means that, despite the good reputation of Goron craftsmanship, most only sell it to individuals who can prove they are unaffiliated with both Royalists and Followers alike.

The Goron system of government is very laid back, with a council of elders overseeing the Goron tribe. They are often represented by a younger leader, usually called ‘Chief’ or ‘Boss’ depending on the leader’s preference. Famously, their Chief during the era of the [[Hero of Time]] became a [[Sages]]. This leader was called [[Sages#Darunia|Darunia]].
