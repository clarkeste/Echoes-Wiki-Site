**The Zuna Republic** is a small island-nation in the southern [[The Great Sea|Great Sea]], near the [[Desert of Doubt|Desert of Doubt]]. It is minor and mostly isolated from the rest of the world culturally, but still engages in [[Economy|trade]] by selling items—particular those found in the tropical Zunari Island, or from the Desert of Doubt—that are rare almost everywhere else in the world. The Zuna Republic is a democracy, and one of the couple of island-nations that have a functioning democracy on a small scale.

The Zuna Republic is inhabited almost exclusively by the Zuna, a unique humanoid species with green skin and thin faces, without a protruding nose. Some may describe them as cactus-like--a reference to their historical origins as a people that roamed and constructed ancient buildings in the deepest reaches of the Desert of Doubt, before migrating to their current home. Style among the Zuna usually includes things such as turbans and flowing robes. Their architecture focuses heavily on the symbol of flowers, and many homes have roof gardens, making their towns and villages particularly green and vibrant with all different colors of flowers.

Starting several hundred years ago, the Zuna population of their island was joined by [[The Gerudo|Gerudo]] from the Desert of Doubt. With the Zuna being the only group nearby to the Gerudo's new exiled home in the Desert of Doubt, the two people became allies, with many marriages between the groups. Many Zuna live around the Gerudo settlements in the Desert of Doubt with their wives, while just as many Gerudo live in the Zuna Republic with their husbands. It is said that the Zuna and Gerudo have a natural kinship, as they both originate from the Desert, and due to how the Gerudo now live in the Zuna ancestral homeland of the Desert of Doubt. Through the Zuna Republic is the only way the Gerudo can interact with the rest of the world, and engage in international trade. The Zuna appreciate this arrangement—not just due to the growing cultural ties between the two peoples, but also due to the economic benefits. The Gerudo, likewise, are grateful to be able to sell their wears and buy important supplies.
