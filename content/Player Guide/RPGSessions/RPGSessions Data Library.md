## The Data Library
This is an excellent feature created by the developer team at [[RPGSessions]] which allows a GM like myself to create a “Kit”. This “Kit” contains all the [[Weapons]], [[Armor]], [[Gear and Equipment|Gear]], and [[Talents]] that are included in the game and that you would want to add to your [[Character]].

So, instead of having to input everything manually, or copy and paste text, you can just see from here you want a [[Talents|Talent]], like [[Grit]], and then search “Grit” in RPGSessions and quickly get it, all already correctly written and in some cases even automated.

Here’s an explanation of how to get it set up, and then how to use it. It’s quite simple to do and will save you a significant amount of time.

#### Step 1: Import the Kit
‘Kits’ on RPGSessions can be shared between users by way of a link. Here’s the link to the Echoes of the Departed Data Library. It will prompt you to import it. Accept, and RPGSessions will know to give you options from the kit.

https://app.rpgsessions.com/data2/kits/import?code=6SzxLuw0FB0e

Click the link. When RPGSession opens, it’ll show you a modal to import the kit. Hit “Find Kit”, as it will usually auto-input the share-code when you click on the link. If it doesn’t auto-fill, input the kit’s share code, and then hit “Find Kit”. The share code is: 6SzxLuw0FB0e

![](https://i.imgur.com/OR7htDe.png)

After hitting “Find Kit”, the modal will show you the Legend of Zelda: Echoes of the Departed kit. Make sure to click “Import Kit”.

![](https://i.imgur.com/ynPDP4Q.png)

The kit should now be imported.

#### Step 2: Assigning a Kit to a Character
After creating or copying a [[Character Sheet]], open it and look for the button pencil button in the top-right corner, below the name and to the right of “More Character Information”.

![](https://i.imgur.com/yuxucVX.png)

After clicking on it, click on the green “Customize Sheet” button near the top-left.

![](https://i.imgur.com/4AcWots.png)

That will open up a modal to modify the Character Sheet. On the left-hand side, below “Game Theme”, there will be an option called “Default Data Library Kits”.

Click there, then add the Data Library kit called “Legend of Zelda: Echoes of the Departed (Import)”. If you can’t find it, just type “Zelda” and it will search for it. If you still can’t find it, made sure you imported it.

Once you’ve done that, hit the blue “update” button in the bottom-right of the modal.

![](https://i.imgur.com/T6IZbAE.png)

### Step 3: Profit!
Now you can add any Weapon, Armor, Gear, or Talent directly from the Data Library! 

The instructions on how to do so are in the [[Items|Item]] article for Items, and in the [[Talents|Talent]] article for Talents—but it’s fairly easy to figure out if you just want to explore the system and what it can do!

## Benefits of the Data Library
Being able to add so many different things to a sheet quickly is incredibly convenient, and is a massive time-saver for both me and [[Player|Players]] alike. Outside of the rare situation where the Data Library isn’t usable for one reason or another, you should generally default to using the Data Library. Not necessarily for browsing things you want to buy—although you can if you want—but for adding them to your sheet instantly and painlessly.

While [[Echoes of the Departed]] has a variety of unlisted [[Items|items]] that must be encountered and found while playing the game, everything listed on the website is also listed in the Data Library. Additionally, [[Talents]] are unchanging. This means it’s particularly invaluable for [[Character Creation Guide|Character Creation]], or whenever you would spend XP.

If you find any issues with EotD Data Library integration, or discrepancies between it and the website, please let me know! I tried to make sure they were as aligned as possible, but it’s possible some things slipped through the cracks.

## Tags
[[RPGSessions]] lets you sort the items in a Data Library by their tags. I’ve compiled a list of the tags I used in the Data Library and their meanings, so you can use them to navigate when relevant.

- **Magic** - Things that are relevant to sorcerers in particular. Almost exclusively used for [[Spell Creation|Magic]] [[Talents]].
- **Affordable** - [[Items]] which can be purchased with the funds avaliable at [[Character Creation Guide|Character Creation]]; ie, Items of 7200 [[Rupees]] in cost or less.
- **Gerudo, Zora, Follower, Royalist, etc** - Denotes the [[Affiliations|Affiliation]] of the [[Items|Item]], as regular [[Affiliations|Affiliation]] does. Usually does not have a mechanical effect, although [[Gerudo]] Items are more exclusive and more suspicious.

## Weapon Categories
In [[RPGSessions]], the “Weapon Category” options can’t be modified, and aren’t one-to-one with EotD’s Weapon categories. However, it should be noted that these categories are purely for organizational purposes and don’t actually appear on the Item anywhere.

Generally speaking, these were the following translations made to Weapon categories:

- Swords/regular one-handed weapons, were added as “Swords”.
- [[Dagger|Daggers]], [[Parrying Dagger|Parrying Daggers]], and [[Gerudo Scimitar|Gerudo Scimitars]] were all added as “Knives”
- Most two-handed weapons, such as claymores or greatswords, were added as “Axes”
- Polearms or spears were added as “Polearms”
- The [[Whip]] was added as a “Whip” (shocker)
- All Shields were added as “Shields” (again, shocking)
- All [[Brawl]] weapons were added as “Gauntlets”
- All Bows except Crossbows were listed as “Bows”
- The [[Hyrulean Crossbow]] and the [[Fighter's Crossbow]] were listed as “Crossbows”
- Weapons in the “utility” folder, such as [[Boomerang|Boomerangs]] or the [[Ball And Chain]], with the exception of the [[Whip]], were added as “Other”
- The [[Bomb (Small)|Bombs]] were listed as “Thrown”
- And finally, artillery weapons such as [[Cannon|Cannons]] were added as “Heavy”.
