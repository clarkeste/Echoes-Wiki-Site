Lakoh, also known as the traitor of the council, served as Minister of the Interior for over half a decade prior to the Revolution. His duties included managing operations and programs inside the Kingdom that did not involve law enforcement, the military, or economic policy--essentially, the day-to-day workings of the Kingdom. He was a mild-mannered, unassuming man, in stark contrast to most of his colleagues, who were either openly boastful, or quietly boastful, such as Chancellor Phurn. While he occasionally butted heads with the Nophandus or Astor on policy, he almost always acquiesced to the votes of the rest of the council.

In the final weeks of Hyrule, Lakoh visited various important cities in an attempt to quell unrest. When he returned, he was ordered to oversee a military march to a suspected Follower stronghold in Karusa Valley; however, this only revealed that the rebels somehow knew of the attack, and fled with ample time to spare.

Recent interviews with Lakoh, the man has stated that in these last days, his doubts on the policy of the King began to increase, believing that a firm hand with the Followers was excessive and authoritarian. Lakoh finally chose to resign his position when the Followers showed off Link Steadfast wielding the Master Sword for all to see, sparking the Revolution. He could, according to him, no longer in good conscious support the government.

Lakoh, therefore, was not in Hyrule Castle and survived, unlike all other members of the Council. He remained in Castle Town during the battle and occupation; and, a week after Castle Town was taken, was appointed as the second Minister-Chairman of the Revolutionary Government, the first being the Confessor. Since then, more has come to light in how he aided the Revolutionaries during and immediately after their attack on the capital, and he has remained among the leadership ranks of the new Hyrulean government. This has caused many Royalists to denounce him as a traitor, and many to suspect that he may have been the elusive Pragmatist, but there is little evidence to suggest he was a spy all along.

In the modern day, Lakoh is among the most influential people in the fledgling new democracy, along with the Confessor--real name still unknown--and the Sorcerer, otherwise known as Agnin Caroline.
