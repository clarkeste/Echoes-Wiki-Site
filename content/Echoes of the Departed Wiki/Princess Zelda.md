Princess Zelda is a very common name for the women of the Hyrulean Royal Family. The most notable Zeldas include the woman who, in legend, is said to have founded Hyrule and been a descendent or incarnation of the Goddess Hylia, the Princess during the era of the Hero of Time, the Princess during the era of the Hero of the Flood, or the last Princess of Hyrule, who died in the revolution.


The Zelda who lived during the era of the Hero of Time was a woman who lived much of her childhood in hiding. Her first nine years of life were normal, even if turbulent; her father and mother, two of the most important people in the realm, attempting to calm the flames of the Hyrulean Civil War. Despite the fact that by the time she was six the war was over, her parents still remained extremely busy, attempting to codify the new peace. Most of Zelda's mentoring was done by her Sheikah nursemaid, whose name has been lost to time.

Only a few years later, however, an even greater tragedy would strike. Using his newfound sway with the King, Ganondorf eliminated him and almost the entirety of the Hyrulean government. Zelda, saved by her bodyguards and nursemaid, were among the only ones to survive. For seven years, she scrounged and stayed in hiding to survive, trying to help where she could. However, after those seven years had passed, Zelda re-connected with her childhood friend, who wielded the Sword of Evil's Bane. Along with the power of seven wise Sages, they were able to defeat Ganondorf and seal him away into the Sacred Realm for one hundred years.

This Zelda gave up her right to the throne shortly after her 18th birthday, before she was to be coronated queen; instead, the throne passed to her brother who had been safe in a distant land. Zelda spent the rest of her life serving as a diplomat between the Hyrulean and Gerudo people, hoping to prevent the conditions that lead to the rise of an individual like Ganondorf to begin with. She died while middle-aged, 53, and never married or had children. Instead, the royal lineage was continued by her brother.


One hundred years later, during the Era of the Flood, another girl named Zelda was born into the Hyrulean Royal Family. She was the granddaughter of the previous Zelda's brother, and unlike her grand-aunt, lived a peaceful childhood. Hyrule was in an era of peace after reconstructing from the vicious seven years of tyrannical rule, and the Hyrulean Civil War before that. She was great friends with the Hero of the Flood, a tradesman from the outskirts of Hyrule who was skilled with a blade, and most agree the two romantically loved one another. However, her life tragically came to a sudden stop when torrential downpours fell from the heavens. She and her father, Daphnes Nohansen, were kidnapped by the newly-returned Ganondorf for the purposes of extracting Zelda's Triforce of Wisdom.

However, Princess Zelda would rather die than hand over the Triforce. And that she did. Under interrogation, she died before giving it over. Ganondorf used and killed Daphnes to draw out the Hero of the Flood, who then avenged them and slew Ganondorf. Some records claim that the Hero confessed that the Demon King allowed him to do this, encouraging it, as he knew it would end the torrential downpour. This theory is often questioned, however, as the most likely cause of the rain was Ganondorf's own magic, which he could have controlled himself.


Twenty-one years ago, the Princess Zelda of the modern day was born. She was kind and beloved by many, and different from her father in many ways.

Zelda spent the majority of her life living in Hyrule Castle and Castle Town. Despite her limited contact for safety reasons, she met and took a liking to a commoner boy, named Link Steadfast, when the two of them were just seven year-old children. However, after knowing each other for a few years, the boy was disallowed from seeing Zelda due to concerns from his station in society and posing a risk to the Princess. Despite this delay, the Princess continued her studies and grew into a young woman. Concerns both from her father and the gossip-mongers ran wild, for she had not yet manifested the Triforce of Wisdom, the birthright of the female members of the Royal Family.

When Link and her were both in adolescence, they met once more when the Princess was allowed to leave the Castle with less supervision. This boy, whose name was Link Steadfast, lived with his father, a blacksmith. Not much is known of his early life, but his destiny became extremely important to the revolution. In a way, Link Steadfast betrayed his childhood friend for ideological convictions when he supported the revolution, the very same individuals that killed his friend.

The details of their relationship is unknown to us. Suffice to say, when the authorities decided that the group that Steadfast belonged had become dangerous, Steadfast fled town along with his sister. As far as know, the two did not meet again.

Six years later, Zelda was present at the fateful Festival of the Flood where the new Ganondorf made his appearance. The arrival of the new Gerudo King caused a stir that caused guards to more closely protect the King and Zelda; however, as it was learned that this Ganondorf was not a violent man, he was brought into the Castle for negotiations along with the King and Zelda. However, once he was there, the Followers of Dragmire conducted a raid on Hyrule Castle, likely emboldened by the presence of a man bearing the same as their god, Ganondorf. Using dozens of secret agents that had infiltrated as soldiers, the Followers infiltrated the Castle. The Sorcerer, the leader of the raid, kidnapped Princess Zelda and defeated all of the King's strongest men, including Astor. However, before she could strike at the King of Hyrule, Ganondorf intervened and saved the King.


However, Zelda was captured. Where exactly she was taken is unknown; however, it is known that the Confessor interrogated her during this time. The Confessor forcefully took the Triforce of Wisdom from Zelda's wrist, who had manifested it in captivity. Later, however, through Zelda's own cunning, she managed to escape her restraints, and met with the Commission for the Recovery of the Golden Power, a group that included her old bodyguard and friend, Impa, and Ganondorf. Despite being encouraged to return to the Castle, she chose to go along with the Commission through their journeys to recover Shards in Zora's Domain and the Lost Woods, successfully helping them. While the Confessor was present to attempt to stop the Commission in the Lost Woods, she and Zelda did not face off once again.

Days later--along with the rest of the Royal Government--Zelda was in Hyrule Castle during the revolution in Kakariko City, and during their march to Castle Town. Whether it was during the assault by the Follower army or by spies inside the Castle, Zelda was killed when an explosive went off, destroying a large part of the Castle and leaving her charred body behind. She was later buried in the Sacred Grounds, a fate better than her father's body, which was burned. Today, Zelda is a rallying cry for the Royalist faction, the martyr for the crimes of the Followers. However, even among the Followers who do not curse her for being a part of the Royal Family, seem to regret her fate, and respect her for supporting the Castle Town Parliamentarians earlier in life.
