The Gerudo are a desert-dwelling people, who have historically been seen as thieves and outlaws. They were the people of Dragmire, although very few Gerudo were directly people in Dragmire's seven year reign. 

The Gerudo have a common ancestor with the Hylians, and have lived in the lands of Hyrule for just as long. At some point, it is believed that the birth rate of Gerudo males fell until it was almost non-existent. As a necessity, trade and cultural exchange increased among the newly-born Kingdom of Hyrule and the Gerudo people. Many Hylians and Gerudo married, both out of necessity and kinship.

During the Hyrulean Civil War, the Gerudo were as split as their Hylian siblings. As the war progressed, most rallied around the leadership of their king, Ganondorf. Under his order, they sided with the victorious Royal Family, gaining a reputation as thieves for their tactics. While Ganondorf’s plans were brutal and drew criticism from his kin despite his birthright, the king of the Gerudo continued. He pledged fealty to the King of Hyrule, who was thankful to the Gerudo. Ganondorf used his newfound raport with the King to move throughout Hyrule freely. With this right, he found the location of the Triforce, a source of near limitless power. Then, he betrayed and killed Hyrule’s King, conquering Castle Town in the process. He then accessed the Triforce, and gained gaining one third of it--the Triforce of Power. With it, he completed his coup d'état and took take over the entire Kingdom for seven long years. Using his influence, he sent his agents across the Kingdom to find King’s daughter, who held another piece of the Triforce, and the young Hero of Time, who bore the final piece.

After Ganondorf empowered himself, the Gerudo joined him. But increasingly their role as soldiers were replaced by monstrous beings who Ganondorf spawned, or who he allured with his power. Castle Town, in its ruined state, was too dangerous for the Gerudo—despite promising his people fairer land, they were relegated to the desert.

After the Hero of Time returned, the majority of the Gerudo began to turn on Ganondorf. Following the lead of one of their ranks, called Nabooru, they ousted Ganondorf from power alongside the Hero of Time, the King’s daughter, and all the other peoples of Hyrule. Despite their role in ending his reign, distrust was sowed between the rest of Hyrule and the Gerudo.

When their former king miraculously returned nearly a century later from his assumed death and was slain after laying waste to the Kingdom, the people of Hyrule chose to exile the Gerudo people for the actions of their former leader. Forced to flee deep into what was named the Desert of Doubt, they were never to return. Their cities, abandoned in the fighting, laid in rubble, as the Hylians began populating the Desert which once belonged to them, sand burying their homes.

Very few of the Gerudo joined together with Dragmire during the Flood. But still, Hyrule Kingdom deemed that the Gerudo were a dangerous people. They invaded the Gerudo Desert in which they resided, and forced the Gerudo people out of their homes. Through a series of bloody battles, the Hylians pushed the Gerudo deep into the desert, and away from civilization. Since then, the Gerudo have rotted in the desert, living by scavenging the desert and living as nomads. Their only neighbor is the island-nation of the Zuna Republic, through which the Gerudo can engage in trade and diplomacy. It is through the Zuna Republic that the Gerudo have any voice in the world stage at all, although both are extremely minor groups, of little consideration by most world leaders.

Gerudo are considered to be an ethnicity of human, much like the Hylians. They tend to have dark skin, amber or green eyes, red hair, a tall-stature, and a sharp nose. However, as with any people, this is not absolute. For example, their skin color, comes in many different tones: like the common light brown and an olive-greenish variation, and lighter tones. Due to a quirk of their biology, the Gerudo have dominant genes; this means the Gerudo are a tribe that consists almost entirely of women, with a male being born only once every hundred years. According to tradition, this male is destined to become their king. When there is no male to be their king, the Gerudo are led by a chief with the position passed on from mother to daughter. The most recent male is Ganondorf, who left his people in the care of a Chief while he traveled to Hyrule, and met his demise at the hands of Link Steadfast. The lack of males of their owne ethnicity means that the Gerudo primarily enter relationships with other peoples. Before, this was Hylians; now, many Gerudo take Zuna husbands. Gerudo names are structured in Eastern name order, with their family name coming before their given name. For example, a Gerudo with the given name Hajei would refer to herself as "Makeela Hajei", with Makeela as the family name. Gerudo have a lifespan nearly identical to that of humans, but with a quicker old age; likely due to the harsh conditions in which they live.


Many rights groups in Hyrule have petitioned the Royal Family to return the Gerudo their lands, or at least to allow them to once more live in Hyrule. However, to this day, this has not happened; the Revolutionary Government has lifted the prohibition, but--despite saying they intend to--have not yet created any program to facilitate this, or formally invited the Gerudo back to their old lands.  Whether the Gerudo are even interested in accepting this Hylian charity after centuries of mistreatment, and the death of Ganondorf, is unknown.
