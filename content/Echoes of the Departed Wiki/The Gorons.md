The Gorons are a large, mountain-dwelling people, who in Hyrule live primarily on and around Death Mountain. They are largely considered a humble and relaxed people, gentle despite their size and Round shape. While they are slow, they can roll into a ball to move faster, their hard skin and back letting them roll even on rough terrain without being bruised or cut.

While the Gorons have become more wary of outsiders since the Civil War, they are still kind to travelers. Refugees and visitors to Death Mountain have become more common. While a tourist destination before, its extremely hot climate dissuaded many. However, after choosing neutrality in the Civil War, Death Mountain has become a hub both for traders, and the wary who have been hurt or dislocated by the war. Despite concerns over housing all of them, the Gorons in large part are more welcoming and kind to these refugees than not.

The primary food of the Gorons are rocks. Usually, the rocks are cooked above red-hot stones, or in lava; creating a Rock Roast, one of the most beloved foods in Goron culture. Of these rocks, Basalt is usually considered the best, but is quite rare. In addition to water, they also drink lava, or make soups with it. Gemstones are nearly universally considered to be a yucky food by the Gorons; supposedly, before they realized that the other peoples of Hyrule would pay top Rupee for it, they used to throw whatever gemstones they gained into the lava of Death Mountain, believing them to be useless. Gorons live an average of a little over one hundred years, and reproduce asexually--this additionally means there is no female gender for the Gorons. Gorons are considered 'brothers' if they were born in the same cave at similar times, and 'descendants' if the line of adoption can be traced back to an 'ancestor'.

During the Hyrulean Civil War, the Gorons have chosen to remain neutral. With their autonomy being violated less than the Zora and Kakariko--aside from some disagreed upon Hyrulean mining operations--the Gorons had little reason to be involved in the Revolution. While individual Gorons chose to help the Followers, Goron society at large did not get involved. During the invasion of Castle Town, the Gorons announced their neutrality, and Death Mountains acts as a zone devoid of both Follower and Royalist soldiers; although spies and diplomats alike both roam Goron City.

The Gorons, not wanting to be involved in violence or cause their home to become simply a launchpad for war profiteering, have banned the sale of weapons to Royalists or Followers on their land. This means that, despite the good reputation of Goron craftsmanship, most only sell it to individuals who are entirely unaffiliated with both Royalists and Followers alike.

The Goron system of government is very laid back, with a council of elders overseeing the Goron tribe.
