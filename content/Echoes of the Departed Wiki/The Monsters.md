While the purpose of the Hyrule Historia is to provide an accounting of historical events in Hyrule and not as a biology textbook, the authors of this book understand the utility in detailing basic facts about some of the most commons monsters in Hyrule. It is believed that monsters are, to a certain extent, immortal. At least during the era of the Demon King, a blood moon would revive all the slain monsters, usually close to where they died.

Bokoblins: Bokoblins are the most type of monster in Hyrule, especially in the centuries without Dragmire. They come in various colors, and seem to have some degree of intelligence. They are clearly a sociable species, as they form together in groups and make makeshift strongholds, and carve clubs from wood--or steal weapons from travelers they attack. They also hunt animals to remain fed.

Moblins: These creatures are form the same family as the Bokoblins, but are much larger--at least two times the size of a Hylian, and a little larger than even a Goron. They often make camp with Bokoblins, and wield much heavier weapons.

Lizalfos: Lizalfos are chameleon-like bipeds who are often described as cunning. They are extremely agile, able to jump large distances, and wield sharp weapons with more finesse than Bokoblins and some Moblins. They will often lie in wait, using their biology to camouflage themselves to their surroundings, and assault any travelers that get too close. There has never been a recorded case of Lizalfos working with Bokoblins or Moblins, outside of some reports during the Great Flood three hundred years ago.

Stalfos: Stalfos are skeletal humanoids, sometimes fallen knights or soldiers, who have been re-animated and attack others. Stalfos usually exist in abandoned structures, in underground caves, or any area where sunlight does not reach, as they seem to actively avoid it. Stalfos often use weapons once made for humans, befitting of their appearance, such as old spears or swords and shields.

Chuchu: Chuchus are water-based monster can be found all over Hyrule, its body acting like gel. It tends to spring its attacks on unsuspecting prey from the ground or from trees. Its strength varies by size, and the type of jelly it drops varies depending on whether the Chuchu was heated up, cooled down, or electrocuted--all things that will also change its appearance and abilities, making the Chuchu more dangerous. Chuchus are slow, and it is advised to flee from them--although smaller blue ones can often be dealt with in a few strikes from even an impromptu weapon.

Keese: Keese are the most common type of bat in Hyrule, and are distinct from other types of their species in that they only have one, large yellow-colored eye. It is debated whether Keese should be characterized as monsters or as an animal, however it is almost certain that it did not come about naturally in the same way many animals did. Keese often fly at night or hang from the ceiling underground, or in dark, abandoned structures. They are weak, and--despite trying to ram into or bite anything that gets too close--can often be felled in one hit. However, Keese may sometimes organize into swarms and attack together, all at once.




No other Monsters are not mentioned in the Hyrule Historia. Zero others are mentioned.
