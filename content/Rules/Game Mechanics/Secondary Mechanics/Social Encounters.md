There are ways to make progress without a bladed edge, however. Whether that be through threats, lies, or simple kindness, it all falls into the category of Social Encounters.

While [[Social Checks|Social Checks]] can be as simple as a [[Skill Checks|Check]] to convince someone of something, a full-length Social Encounter is much more in-depth.

While most persuading will only require a single Check, some more important or in-depth negotiations may require a series of checks. This uses the systems stated in [[Progress & Countdown Clocks]], but specifically tailored for a duel of wits, convincing someone of something particularly complex, poking holes in their beliefs, or otherwise an attempt to convince them that will take a long time. These are most often called **Social Encounters**.

The core of Social Encounters are attempting to fill a Progress Clock, which represents the [[Character|Character(s)]] who are the targets of the [[Encounter|Encounter]] becoming convinced or aligned with the Character who is trying to persuade them.

This is done by using various [[Approach|Approaches]] to try and convince them. An Approach is a specific line of argumentation, or tactic. Different Approaches have a different [[Social Skills|Social Skill]] attached to them, and different Approaches will appeal to different people depending on the circumstance. A good Approach will result in up to 5 [[Boost Dice]]; a bad one will result in up to 5 [[Setback Dice]]. Use the Approaches as a sort of warm/cold meter to figure out what resonates with this [[NPCs|NPC]] the most, and unearth how you can convince them before it’s too late. 

Will your Approach be based on Ugly Truths, Soothing Platitudes, Etiquette, clear Falsehoods, etc? Which will resonate the best with the current target?

Social Encounters do not necessarily take place all in one scene. A [[Character]]’s Progress Clock to be convinced can be partially filled at one time due to one Check, and then picked up later on.

#### Beginning a Social Encounter
[[The GM]] chooses when a Social Encounter is started. Beginning a Social Encounter is dependent on two things: It is something that is difficult to convince the target of, and therefore cannot be decided in a single Check; and, it is something they can indeed be affected by.

As with regular Social Checks, there is still a range of possibilities, and the target will not necessarily believe anything even on success. However, even in the most mild of cases, it will represent a doubt or stress that eats at them.

Just like other Progress Clocks, Social Encounters are won by filling all the Segments on a Progress Clock. Usually, a Success on a Check will ‘Tick’ the Progress Clock forward, and fill a Segment. The amount of Segments is set by the GM at the beginning of the Social Encounter. This is determined by how resistant a certain character is to the idea being presented to them. If the resistance would cause over 12 Segments on the Clock, convincing that Character is usually considered impossible.

#### Engaging in a Social Encounter
After a Social Encounter has begun, the offending character—usually a [[PC|PC]]—makes the first check. Each Check must have a different [[Approach|Approach]]—this is the different ways that a Character tries to convince their target. The same Approach can never be repeated. 

##### Approaches and Social Checks
*Main article - [[Approach]]*

Approaches are the core of Social Encounters. An Approach refers to a [[Skills|Skill]] in conjunction with an argument. For instance, if a PC were trying to convince the King to spare a criminal on death row, he could attempt to claim that the criminal was innocent. “Perhaps,” the King says. “But he has been sentenced.” The PC can now no longer claim that the criminal was innocent as an Approach. Instead, he must change gears to a different argument, or he must attempt to show the King why the sentencing was fraudulent, biased, or incorrect, thereby building on what was previously said.

Giving compromises or other such things can often benefit an Approach greatly, even beyond the original benefits. But you are expected to be held to these things.

Using a different Skill is automatically a new Approach. Attempting to convince the King that the criminal was innocent using your oratory skills would be [[Charm|Charm]]; however, if you did it again, this time using [[Coercion|Coercion]] to tell him that the criminal was innocent, and once truth would come out his justice system would be tarnished, it would be a different Approach.

The [[Difficulty|Difficulty]], in every case, will be an [[Opposed Check|Opposed Check]]. This means the Difficulty will be equal to the target’s [[Cool|Cool]], [[Vigilance|Vigilance]], or [[Discipline|Discipline]], depending on the Skill used to convince them. Perhaps the King has a high Cool and is resistant to Charm, but his Vigilance is low—meaning he is likely to believe lies with sufficient plausibility.

Each new Approach can gain up to five [[Setback Dice|Setbacks]] or five [[Boost Dice|Boosts]] depending on the quality of the argument made, with some more extreme bonuses or debuffs existing. For example, if the King cares little for the opinions of his people, convincing him that the reputation of his justice system would be tarnished by the false conviction would suffer Setbacks, as it is something he does not care much about. Likewise, appealing to his glory by telling him that he could put the judiciary in their place by overturning their conviction with solid evidence, would gain Boosts. Attempt to discover the weaknesses and strengths of a character, and what arguments do or do not work. Some Approaches, if entirely irrelevant, cannot be used.

In some cases, [[Knowledge Skills|Knowledge Skills]] may be usable, not just [[Social Skills|Social Skills]]. However, Knowledge Skills do not necessarily count as a new Approach.

If an Approach particularly appeals or prays on a Character’s Belief, or some other great facet such as their greatest Fears, the GM may say that the Social Check adds an automatic [[Success]]. If it particularly against their Belief or Fear, it may sometimes result in an automatic [[Failure]].
###### Exchanges
It should be noted that an Approach is *not* a single line of dialogue or argumentation, which is then rolled for, and determines the NPC’s response.

Approaches are, very often, longer exchanges, and then a Social Check is rolled at the end of the exchange to determine if got through or not. Interlocutors will often try to rebuff or rebut an Approach to test and challenge it, and a Character’s defense of it can impact the Effectiveness of the Approach, and how many Boost or Setback Dice are added.

If an exchange goes on for long enough and covers different potential Approach topics, then Success on that Check could mean filling 2, or even 3, Segments on a Progress Clock. On a Failure, it might still add Boosts to the next Social Check.

#### Winning a Social Encounter
There are two victory states for a Social Encounter.
- The PCs tick over the Progress Clock enough times to fill all Segments.
- The targeted [[NPCs|NPC]] goes over their [[Strain Threshold|Strain Threshold]].

#### Losing a Social Encounter
There are five failure states for a Social Encounter.
- The PCs have exhausted all viable Approaches that they know of, and have failed to adequately convince their target in those areas. Anything they say now would simply be repeating themselves.
- The PCs have greatly offended or frustrated their target, usually through a [[Despair|Despair]], and were not able to salvage the conversation. This naturally leads to the target no longer entertaining the conversation.
- Some Social Encounters have Countdown Clock that, upon being filled, ends the Social Encounter. The Countdown Clock can Tick due to time, Failed Checks, Checks by the interlocutor opponent, etc.
- A participating PC goes over their [[Strain Threshold|Strain Threshold]].

#### Counter-Checks
Every time a [[Character]] rolls their [[Skill Checks|Check]], their interlocutor (ie, a person they are trying to convince) also gets a ‘Turn’. They can either skip this turn, and heal 1 [[Strain]] in the process, or they can choose to suffer 2 Strain to make a Counter-Check and roll their own [[Social Checks|Social Check]]. If successful, it inflicts Strain equal to Successes on their target.

Some Social Encounters may instead be more **symmetrical**. This means that the [[NPCs]] also have a Progress Clock they are trying to fill, to convince the PCs of something, or to show them that the NPCs are unflinching in their resolve, or to tire the PCs. If this occurs, they are bound to the same rules as the PCs (and have the same turn order) and cannot inflict [[Strain]] directly, just like their opponents.

#### The End of a Social Encounter
At the end of a Social Encounter, the PCs will either have successfully gained enough Successes to be victorious—thereby usually convincing the target or leaving them without an argument—or they will have lost, due to the various possible failure states.

If they fail, the target will often be annoyed or adversarial, but no ill mechanical effects will befall the PCs aside from the loss of resources they used in the Encounter. However, should they succeed, the NPC’s opinion will either have been changed, influenced in such a way that will have consequences later, or they will suffer heavy debuffs in relevant situations as a result of the questions raised.

#### Social Encounters against Groups
Social Encounters don’t have to just target one person—they can be against multiple people, or a crowd. Dealing with a crowd is very similar to the rules for dealing with a single person, except with more unpredictable Approaches.

Dealing with a smaller number, however, such as 2-3, is different. The amount of Segments on a Progress Clock required, in this case, represents different individuals combined. When enough Ticks on the Progress Clock for one character is reached, they will often recuse themselves.
  
Some characters cannot be convinced of closely-held beliefs in a group and will need to be approached separately.
