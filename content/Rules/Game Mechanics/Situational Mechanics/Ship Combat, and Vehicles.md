There are different rules for handling water-faring Ships and larger vehicles such as Carriages, chariots, or other similar contraptions.

At the core of these rules, are the Ship/vehicle sheets, which are like [[Character Sheet|Character Sheets]] but instead of describing the abilities of a [[Character]], it describes the properties and abilities of a Ship or vehicle. While some of these properties are identical to those found in Character Sheets, such as [[Soak]], others are a little different.

### Ship and vehicle Sheets
#### Integrity Threshold 
The **Integrity Threshold** of a Ship or vehicle is akin to its [[Wound Threshold]]. It determines how much physical [[Damage]] it can take from [[Attack Check|Attacks]] and other damage to its hull and frame. Aside from that, the Integrity Threshold functions the same as the Wound Threshold on a regular [[Character]].

Just like the Wound Threshold of a Character, when a Ship or vehicle passes its Integrity Threshold, it becomes unusable. If it is akin to a [[Adversary#Minions|Minion]] or [[Adversary#Rivals|Rival]], then the Ship or vehicle sinks, falls apart, explodes, or is otherwise destroyed. Otherwise, it suffers a Ship [[Critical Injury]] and becomes inoperable due to major damage that could destroy it if quick action is not taken.

Integrity can be [[Operating|repaired using the Operating]] [[Skills|Skill]].

#### Strain Threshold
The **Strain Threshold** of a Ship or vehicle is largely identical to the [[Strain Threshold]] of a regular [[Character]]. It represents pushing a Ship or vehicle to its limit. The creaking of wood, loosening of important parts, quality of the rope, mast, or tiller, and things that could cause a Ship to fall apart, but that are separate from the Integrity of the hull or frame. 

Just like the Strain Threshold of a Character, it can be used to activate various [[Talents]] or [[Maneuvers]]. Additionally, when the active amount of Strain goes over the Strain Threshold, the ship slows its Speed to 0 and is unable to move.

Strain on a Ship or vehicle can be [[Operating|repaired using the Operating]] [[Skills|Skill]] or other actions done by the crew. Unlike regular [[Strain]] on a Character, it cannot be healed using [[Advantage]].

#### Soak
The **Soak** of a Ship or vehicle is identical to the [[Soak]] of a regular [[Character]]. It represents the armor and defenses of a vehicle or ship against [[Attack Check|Attacks]]. 

Just like regular Soak, all incoming [[Damage]] is reduced equal to the amount of [[Soak]] the vehicle or Ship suffering the Damage has.

#### Defense
The **Defense** of a Ship or vehicle is, again, identical to the [[Defense]] of a regular Character. It represents even more significant armor or maneuverability that has the chance to deflect attacks entirely.

Just like regular Defense, all [[Attack Check|Attack Checks]] targeting a Ship or vehicle with Defense add [[Setback Dice]] equal to the vehicle’s Defense rating when targeting it.

#### Handling
**Handling** is a value on a Ship or vehicle that determines how easy it is for the driver or Captain to maneuver and control it. It can be caused by a wide variety of factors, such as an effective handling system, or a bulky frame.

The Handling of a Ship or vehicle adds [[Boost Dice]] equal to the positive Handling rating any [[Operating]] [[Skill Checks]] to control the Ship or vehicle. If the Handling rating is instead negative (ie, -2), then [[Setback Dice]] equal to the negative Handling rating are added to any [[Operating]] [[Skill Checks|Skill Check]] to control the Ship or vehicle.

#### Silhouette
**Silhouette** is simply how big or small the vessel in question is, using the [[Silhouette]] system used in the rest of [[Echoes of the Departed]].

To read more about it and how it can impact [[Difficulty]] in [[Combat]], check out the aforementioned [[Silhouette|Silhouette article]].

#### Maximum Speed
The most unique part of Ships/Vehicles and their Combat is **Maximum Speed**, and the current Speed they happen to be traveling at. The Maximum Speed determines how fast a vessel can go, but any Ship or vehicle can go at a Speed less than their Maximum Speed. However, outside of specific circumstances in which a Captain or operator pushes their vessel to its breaking point, a Ship or vehicle cannot go faster than its Maximum Speed. Maximum Speed is usually between 1-5.

During [[Combat]], the Operator, Captain, or whoever is driving or managing a Ship or Vehicle gains [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuvers]] equal to the current Speed of the Ship or Vehicle. They **must** spend all Ship Maneuvers. 

### Encumbrance Capacity
This determines how many people or objects a Ship or vehicle can transport. Usually this can be ignored and treated as “a lot”. But in some cases, it might be relevant.

For the purposes of determining [[Encumbrance]] on a ship, each [[Silhouette]] 1 person is 5 Encumbrance, with each additional Silhouette adding +5. Silhouette 0 individuals are 2 Encumbrance.

### Crew Required
Larger Ships require a significant crew complement to keep them running. Not everything has a vital or exciting job; many are just there to keep an eye on things, or operate functions such as the anchor or carrying out orders on the mast. Many Ships or Vehicles, like a simple Sailboat or a Chariot, will only require a single person to operate it.


### Structured Time and Combat
Just as with there are additional rules in [[Combat]] without Vehicles, there are additional rules that apply to Ships and vehicles when they are in Combat. While many of these rules, such as [[Attack Check|Attack Checks]] remain the same with regular Combat, Ships and vehicles tend to move in different ways than their bipedal counterparts.

It should be noted that the addition of vehicles does not change what individual [[Character|Characters]] can do in Combat or structured time. Even in an [[Encounter|Encounter]] heavily featuring Ships and or vehicles, a Character can still do any of the [[Maneuvers]], [[Actions|Actions]], and [[Incidentals]] they usually could. This can be things such as moving around the deck of a Ship, or shooting Arrows out of a carriage window. The main difference in Ship and Vehicle Combat comes in how the vessels themselves move, how their resources are managed, and how Ship-bound [[Weapons]] are operated.

#### Changing Speed
Out-of-combat, changing Speed is fairly easy and painless, and can simply be declared like anything else. In [[Combat]] or an otherwise dangerous situation, however, it becomes a much more tenuous proposition.

Each Round, on the turn of the Captain, Pilot, Operator, or the person otherwise managing the vessel, the aforementioned person can change the Speed of the vessel, as an [[Incidentals|Incidental]]. They can increase or decrease the Speed by 1. Each additional increase or decrease of Speed takes [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Strain Threshold|Ship Strain]]. For instance, increasing or decreasing Speed by 3 causes the Ship to suffer 2 Strain.

#### Ship Maneuvers
During [[Combat]], the Operator, Captain, or whoever is driving or managing a Ship or Vehicle, is the [[Character]] who decides how **Ship Maneuvers** are spent. A Ship gains Maneuvers equal to the current Speed of the Ship or Vehicle. They **must** spend all Ship Maneuvers on their turn. Ship Maneuvers are unrelated to the [[Maneuvers]] used by regular Characters.

Ship Maneuvers can be spent on the following things:

##### Move
Move between locations and [[Range Bands|Range Bands]] using the same rules as in [[Maneuvers#Move|the Move Maneuver]] for individual [[Character|Characters]]. Additionally, [[Strategic Range]] can be reached by spending three Move Maneuvers from [[Extreme Range|Extreme]] to Strategic.

Despite using the same [[Range Bands|Range Bands]], it should be noted that the Range Bands in a Ship-based [[Encounter]] usually represents larger quantities of space than usual.

If the vessel’s Captain, rider, or helmsman chooses to use the Move Maneuver to take their Ship through a particularly dangerous or precarious course, [[The GM|the GM]] may require that they make an Operating or Riding [[Skill Checks|Check]], depending on the type of vehicle or mount, to successfully navigate.

##### Evade
With a [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuver]], may Evade by taking evasive action. When doing so, select a number no higher than the Ship’s [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Strain Threshold|Strain Threshold]]. The Ship then suffers an amount of Strain equal to the number selected. All incoming [[Damage]] targeting the Ship or vehicle using Evade is reduced by the same amount. However, due to the unsteady nature of the vessel’s movements, all [[Attack Check|Attacks]] originating from the vessel that used Evade has its Damage reduced by the same amount as well. Essentially, trade accuracy for the ability to avoid incoming fire.

Cannot take the Evade and [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Strike|Strike]] Ship Maneuver in the same turn, and cannot benefit from them both simultaneously.

##### Strike
With a [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuver]], may use Strike by repositioning the vessel for best use of firepower. When doing so, select a number no higher than the Ship’s [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Strain Threshold|Strain Threshold]]. The Ship then suffers an amount of Strain equal to the number selected. All [[Attack Check|Attack Checks]] originating from this vessel have their [[Damage]] increased by an equal amount. However, due to the risky nature of the vessel’s maneuvers, all Attacks targeting the Ship or Vehicle using Strike have the [[Damage]] they inflict onto the vessel increased by the same amount. Essentially, trade defense for the chance to inflict greater damage to the enemy vessel.

Cannot take the [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Evade|Evade]] and Strike Ship Maneuver in the same turn, and cannot benefit from them both simultaneously.

##### Brace For Impact
When using Brace For Impact, requiring a [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuver]], suffer any number of ship [[Strain]]. Until the beginning of the Captain, helmsman, or operator’s turn, ignore all incoming [[Damage]] up to the amount of Strain suffered. This represents maneuvering a Ship or vehicle to reduce damage to the vessel.

##### Collide 
The Collide [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuver]] may only be done if the active Ship or vehicle ‘has the Advantage’ by completing the [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Gain The Advantage|Gain The Advantage]] [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Actions|Action]]. Additionally, the Ship or vehicle must be within [[Engaged Range]] of the target of Collide.

When using Collide, the target Ship is dealt [[Damage]] equal to ten times (10x) the [[Silhouette]] difference between the ramming vessel and the defending vessel, plus an additional 30 Damage. The vessel that used the Collide Ship Maneuver then suffers half the Damage inflicted.

##### Prepare to Board
As a [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuver]], when within [[Engaged Range]] of another Ship or vehicle, may take the Prepare to Board Ship Maneuver. The vessel in question repositions itself so that it is flush with the other ship, allowing individuals to jump across with an [[Difficulty|Average]] (2p) [[Difficulty]] Coordination or Athletics [[Skill Checks|Check]], as an [[Actions|Action]].


#### Ship Actions
Just like in ground-scale [[Combat]] there are [[Actions]], so are there Actions that can impact an entire Ship or vehicle. These are called Ship Actions, similar to [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuvers]].

However, whereas Ship Maneuvers were independent from regular [[Maneuvers]] and instead based on Speed, Ship Actions are not. The [[Character]] in charge—the helmsman, operator, captain, or otherwise manager of the vessel—must use their regular [[Actions|Action]] to take a Ship Action when controlling the vessel.

Therefore, Characters can usually only take one Ship Action per turn. However, their impacts are usually more significant than that of Ship Maneuvers. The following below are examples of what Ship Actions can taken.

##### Gain The Advantage
As a [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Actions|Ship Action]], may attempt to Gain The Advantage. This represents a Ship or Vehicle maneuvering into a more advantageous position. With a sailing Ship, this might mean maneuvering to be upwind of a target, but with a carriage or horse, it might mean tailing your enemy closely.

When executing Gain The Advantage, pick one target. The active [[Character]] then make an Operating or Riding [[Skill Checks|Check]] (depending on the skill used by the vehicle or mount) with [[Difficulty]] determined by the Speed difference between the two vessels. If the Speed between the two vessels is equal, the [[Difficulty]] is Easy (1p). If the Speed of your vessel is above that of the target, then the Difficulty is Average (2p). If the Speed of your vessel is *lower* than that of the target, increase the Difficulty, starting from Easy, by each point of Speed your vessel is slower.

If the initiating vessel or mount succeeds, they gain ‘The Advantage’ over the target. When they have this benefit, [[Dice Upgrade|upgrade]] all [[Attack Check|Attack Checks]] from this vessel targeting the other vessel by two. If a vessel attacks another Ship or vehicle that has ‘The Advantage’ over them, then that vessel upgrades the [[Difficulty]] twice.

Essentially, for the vessel that used Gain The Advantage, attacking their enemy is easier, and it is harder for that enemy to attack them back.

##### Concentrated Barrage
Concentrated Barrage is a [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Actions|Ship Action]]. Unlike most other Ship Actions and [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuvers]], any individual onboard the vessel in a position of leadership or authority can do Concentrated Barrage. To do Concentrated Barrage, the vessel must have at least four different [[Weapons]] on board it can fire, and must select at least two.

The [[Character]] who is doing Concentrated Barrage then rolls an [[Attack Check]], using the either the [[Skills|Skill]] used by the majority of the [[Weapons]], or by using the [[Leadership]] Skill. If the Check is successful, the [[Damage]] of one of the Weapons is applied. Each additional uncanceled [[Advantage]] on the check can then add the [[Damage#Base Damage|Base Damage]] of an additional Weapon used in the Attack, once for each Weapon. This represents the other Weapons successfully hitting their target.

As with regular Attacks, once a Weapon has been used in Concentrated Barrage—even if they do not successfully hit—it cannot be used again for the rest of the Round.

##### Focus Fire!
Focus Fire! is a [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Actions|Ship Action]]. Unlike most other Ship Actions and [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuvers]], any individual onboard the vessel in a position of leadership or authority can execute this Action.

When using Focus Fire! make a **[[Difficulty|Hard]]** (3p) **[[Leadership]]** [[Skill Checks|Check]] and select one part of a target as if you were making a [[Maneuvers#Called Shot|Called Shot]] [[Maneuvers|Maneuver]]. If successful, all [[Attack Check|Attack Checks]] originating from that same vessel may fire and damage that part of the target as if they had done a Called Shot Maneuver. The same penalties apply.


#### Sailing Upwind

##### What does “Upwind” mean?
Upwind has been referenced a few times by now. I struggled to understand what it meant, so I wanted to put as simple a description as possible here.

“Upwind” means *towards* the origin of the Wind; ie, the opposite direction it is blowing. If I say a vessel is upwind of us, that means it is towards the origin of the wind from us. Because of this, a Ship that is Upwind can move *with* the Wind, in the direction the wind is blowing, to get to its target. Downwind is when a Ship *cannot*. 

Downwind is when a Ship is *away* from the origin of the wind (ie, the direction the Wind is blowing). Downwind is fine for running away, but it means it’s harder to engage the enemy. This is because going towards the enemy to engage them would be going *against* the wind, and therefore be harder.

This is not directly modeled in [[Echoes of the Departed]], outside of situations where the wind is very strong. Instead, the [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Gain The Advantage|Gain The Advantage]] [[Actions|Action]] tends to represent this. If a Ship takes the Gain The Advantage Action, they maneuver in such a way that they are upwind of the enemy ship.

##### Sailing towards the Wind
In some causes where the wind is particularly strong, the [[The GM|GM]] may rule that sailing *towards* the wind (ie, sailing upwind) costs twice as many [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Ship Maneuvers]]  as it usually would. This is akin to [[Difficult Terrain]]. Ships moved by oars, steam engines, or any source of momentum besides sails and masts, cannot suffer from this drawback.

When this is done, Gain The Advantage has the added effect of maneuvering the vessel into being closer to the direction of the incoming wind than the target vessel. Ie, the Ship using Gain The Advantage is “upwind” of their enemy.

This is already usually implied in the narrative, but is codified into the rules in the cases of when the wind is so strong it has a tangible mechanical impact.

### The Crew
While the Captain or helmsman of a Ship, or the driver of a Carriage, is doing their job, there are often others on the vehicle in question. Despite not directly maneuvering the Ship or vehicle, these individuals often must contribute in vital ways to keep the Ship afloat, or alive during [[Combat|combat]].

Everything a [[Character]] can do in regular Combat, they can do when on a Ship or vehicle. This includes all [[Maneuvers]], [[Actions]], and [[Incidentals]] they would usually have access to, such as the ability to move on the deck of a Ship. A leader could, for example, inspire the gunners, or a medic could heal the wounded.

In addition to the regular options previously mentioned, there are some other things a Character might be doing on a Ship more often than otherwise, and are usually important duties on a Ship or vehicle.

#### Manning Weapons
Many Ships or vehicles have [[Weapons]], such as [[Cannon]], embedded onto the vessel. Any Character can use one of these Weapons by being within [[Engaged Range]] of them, and spending a Maneuver to man them. 

Then, as with any Weapon they hold, they can make an [[Attack Check]], targeting a vessel or Character within [[Range Bands|Range]] of the Weapon. This uses the same method of determining [[Difficulty]] as a regular Attack Check, although it should be noted that Difficulty modifiers due to [[Silhouette]] are more common in vehicle combat.

Called Shots are also particularly valuable in Ship Combat, allowing gunners to. target particular systems or parts of a vehicle. This could be a mast on a sailing ship, or the wheels on a chariot.

When a Weapon on a Ship or vehicle, it cannot be fired again until the next Round, even if a new Character attempts to use it.

#### Damage Control
Repairing Ships or vehicles, throwing flooding water overboard, jury-rigging damaged systems; these all fall into the purview of Damage Control.

Similar to [[Medicine|Medical Checks]], [[Craftsmanship]] can be used to heal [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Strain Threshold|Strain]], or [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Integrity Threshold|Integrity]] on a Ship or vehicle. On larger vessels, a [[Character]] will often be required to move between points on the Ship that are damaged, but will also be allowed to do the Damage Control [[Actions|Action]] more often. Each particular place where the Ship is damaged can only benefit from one Damage Control Action, until it is damaged again. Usually, the GM decides the location of the damage.

Damage Control operates identically to Medical Checks, with [[Success|Successes]] healing Integrity and [[Advantage]] healing Strain. In addition to this, the masts and other important components of the ship can be the target of an endless amount of [[Craftsmanship]] Checks even if they are not damaged, but these Checks only heal Strain. [[Success|Successes]] gained on them heal [[Strain]], as do [[Advantage]].

In particularly dire circumstances where there are multiple hull breaches but the ship is still salvageable, keeping a sea-faring Ship afloat may become a [[Skill Challenges|Skill Challenge]].

#### Spotting
May make an [[Difficulty|Average]] (2p) [[Perception]] [[Skill Checks|Check]] to a spot a weak-point or error in the formation or direction of the enemy. If successful, add two [[Boost Dice]] to the next [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Actions|Ship Action]] preformed.

#### Boarding and Fighting Boarders
Boarding another vessel, usually to capture it or disable it from the inside if other methods prove unreliable, is not an uncommon tactic. If a Ship or vehicle has done the [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Prepare to Board|Prepare to Board]] [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Ship Maneuvers|Maneuver]], this is a simple [[Difficulty|Average]] (2p) [[Difficulty]] Coordination or Athletics [[Skill Checks|Check]], as an [[Actions|Action]].

However, if they have not, then this requires a significant jump. Usually, to land on the deck of the other ship, one must be jumping from within [[Engaged Range]] and make a [[Difficulty|Daunting]] (4p) Athletics or Coordination [[Skill Checks|Check]], as an Action. In some cases, a [[Character]] might be able to jump from further range, but it’s unlikely that they would land successfully on the deck or top of the vehicle. Additionally, some sea-faring Ships have smaller vessels dedicated for boarding, and can be manned by the crew independently.

However, the enemy too can board a friendly Ship or vehicle. In this case, it becomes the duty of those onboard to thwart the attackers.

### Repairing
Unfortunately, repairing a Ship or vehicle is much harder than attempting to heal a living being. Due to the lack of natural processes for healing, a Ship or vehicle usually requires dedicated facilities to put together broken parts. And that is if a damaged Ship can survive long enough at sea to arrive at a port, or if a carriage can hold together long enough to get to a city.

As outlined in [[Ship Combat, and Vehicles#Damage Control|Damage Control]], a Ship or vehicle can only be healed with a [[Craftsmanship]] [[Skill Checks|Check]] once for each attack it suffered. The maximum ‘backlog’ for [[Craftsmanship]] [[Skill Checks|Checks]] that can be made is three. Additionally, a [[Critical Injury]] can only be attempted to heal once, though with a [[Sacred Points|Sacred Point]] and a sufficiently different situation, the [[The GM|GM]] may allow another chance.

To heal beyond that, [[Downtime|days, weeks, or even month]] are required in a port or dockyard, with proper facilities to provide major repairs to a Ship. With other types of vehicle, specialized and significant facilities and tools are still required to properly repair the damage done.
