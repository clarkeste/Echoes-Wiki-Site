Enchant an [[Items|Item]] using any Spell you have discovered. Simply use a Spell you wish to put on an item in conjunction with Enchant. This applies your Spell to that Item, if it be a crystal, rod, or sword. 

With this, even non-Spellcasters can take advantage of that power. This is the process by which items such as the Fire Rod, the Cane of Somaria, and some Fireblades were made. Can also be used to Unenchant items.

Anyone who uses an Enchanted Item must still suffer the regular 2 [[Strain]] to cast a [[Spell Creation|Spell]], and must have the Enchanted Item in one of their hands. Likewise, if an Item is Enchanted with an [[Endow]] Spell that has a range of [[Engaged Range|Engaged]], that Enchanted Item leaving Engaged Range of its target and user will make the Spell dissipate. However, [[Characters]] who use an Enchanted Item do not need to have the requisite Magic Points, and do not need to have ranks in [[Force]]—although they still roll Force to determine the success of the Spell.

In addition to the main Spell that Enchant is used in conjunction with to put an Enchantment on an Item, Modifiers can also be placed on Enchantment separately. It is not entirely clear this does, if it does anything at all.

Its default [[Range Bands|Range]] is [[Engaged Range|Engaged]]. 

An Item can only be Enchanted once, unless it is first unenchanted with a second Enchantment spell. While some legends speak of locations where an Item could be Enchanted multiple times, there is no evidence of this.

If an [[Encumbrance]] 0 Item is Enchanted, it immediately becomes Encumbrance 1.
