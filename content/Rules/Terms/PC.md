PC stands for ‘Player Character’. It encompasses every [[Character|Character]] played by a [[Player|Player]]. All PCs require full [[Character Sheet|Character Sheets]], and must be created using the [[Character Creation Guide|Character Creation]] process. PCs, being the way that a Player interfaces with the world, are extremely important to [[Echoes of the Departed]]—and are, almost without exception, the protagonists of the story.

### Maximum Active Characters
Each [[Player]] can have multiple Characters. However, for the sake of not getting too many unable to be tracked at any given time, there are some limits to be mentioned.

A Player can only have *three* Activated PCs at any given time. What is an Activated PC? Well, it is a PC that the Player is currently playing, and which is currently engaging in the game in some way.

For example, if a Player has three PCs and wants to start playing a fourth, they must temporarily retire one of those PCs. Story-wise, this can simply mean they experience some [[Downtime]] off-screen that gets resolved whenever they are Activated again. PCs that are currently not in use are called Inactive PCs.

### Inactive PCs
PCs can become inactive in a few ways. By a conscious choice from the [[Player]] to temporarily or permanently retire them; by their death, or their extended capture, although only rarely.

Another common way is that, despite not stating it outright, the [[Player]] behind the [[Character]] has simply not made any comment or effort with their Character for an extended period of time; usually one to two weeks. In this case, [[The GM]] may rule that the PC is now an Inactive PC.

This may seem a little harsh. However, it is necessary on my part to keep my mental load of the game to a manageable level. Abandoning without forewarning is often not intentional and not malicious, of course, but having that open thread takes up a significant amount of mental bandwidth for no purpose or benefit to everyone, essentially serving only to vex the GM. The onus of getting everyone up to speed on what happened prior and making sure the details are remembered by the PC essentially always falls on the GM, and so the GM is usually the one that reaps the consequences and stress of this.

### What Happens When a PC is Inactive?
Not that much! As we’ve gone over previously, an Inactive PC is simply not being played or in a plotline. So long as they are not dead or otherwise [[Incapacitated|incapacitated]], they can be picked up again by their [[Player]].

The main effects are the following:
- An Inactive PC does not count towards the ‘Active PC total’ mentioned previously
- When an Inactive PC becomes Active, there is usually a time-jump and large scene change, like an ending to an episode in an episodic series. This is even if the PC did not become Inactive on an ending point, again so that the GM’s stress is not excessively increased.
- If a PC became Inactive because their [[Player]] did not engage with the game for a long period of time, [[The GM]] may wait a period of time until they can be sure the [[Player]] is active again before allowing the PC to become active again.

If all of the PCs belonging to a [[Player]] become Inactive for long enough, the Player will be considered an Inactive Player. They are essentially a spectator, but can re-join at some point later on, so long as they can commit to avoiding a similar level of inactivity that brought them to that point, if their PCs were made Inactive by default due to a lack of engagement.
